    Reflections on Personal Yoga Practice

    Reflections on personal Yoga practice: By sheer coincidence, I started trying Yoga during the pandemic in 2020. I purchased a Keep membership and followed their Hot Yoga sessions. After sticking with it for a few years, I can now perform many asanas quite well. Personally, I feel the guidance provided by video instructors often exceeds that of many in-person training classes. I follow a simple logic: any Yoga instructor wants to put their best side, the most detailed explanations, and the most refined content into their videos. Furthermore, everyone tends to slack off a bit at work. A single in-person class usually yields a relatively low real-world return for them, so few instructors actually enter a classroom with the intention of ensuring students get their money’s worth. Often, classes turn into opportunities for the teacher to show off or engage in small talk. However, in video preparation, they gain higher returns due to the principle of leverage. Even if not for money, but for the sake of reputation and long-term development, they are bound to cut the fluff and present their most professional selves. I believe the same logic applies to other training, such as learning German. High-quality, refined, and systematic online courses are not easy to prepare. In an offline class, you simply watch the students’ reactions; they might be there because of the teacher’s fame, or simply because it’s convenient—close by, right time, right location, etc. However, online courses inevitably compete with many other high-quality offerings. If one doesn’t take it seriously, it’s impossible to gain the trust of followers. If you lack unique insights or concise summaries, you might only succeed through the first-mover advantage, aesthetics, filming quality, or the sheer volume of videos. ...

    Observing the Chain Reaction of My Parents' Emotions

    Dad likes to focus on the future; he is concerned with things like Musk, Starlink, commemorative notes, AI, emerging trends, and making money. This is the only form of success recognized by his values. He demands this from those around him, and he demands it of himself. Therefore, when those around him fail to meet these expectations, or when he fails himself, his frustration turns into rage. He wants to control things so they go exactly as he expects. But when he encounters things he cannot understand or control, he becomes angry. Getting angry is a manifestation of having no other choices. If we had other ways to handle embarrassment, we could simply use self-deprecation or encouragement to face it calmly.
