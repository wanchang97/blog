    Reflections on personal Yoga practice: By sheer coincidence, I started trying Yoga during the pandemic in 2020. I purchased a Keep membership and followed their Hot Yoga sessions. After sticking with it for a few years, I can now perform many asanas quite well. Personally, I feel the guidance provided by video instructors often exceeds that of many in-person training classes. I follow a simple logic: any Yoga instructor wants to put their best side, the most detailed explanations, and the most refined content into their videos. Furthermore, everyone tends to slack off a bit at work. A single in-person class usually yields a relatively low real-world return for them, so few instructors actually enter a classroom with the intention of ensuring students get their money’s worth. Often, classes turn into opportunities for the teacher to show off or engage in small talk. However, in video preparation, they gain higher returns due to the principle of leverage. Even if not for money, but for the sake of reputation and long-term development, they are bound to cut the fluff and present their most professional selves. I believe the same logic applies to other training, such as learning German. High-quality, refined, and systematic online courses are not easy to prepare. In an offline class, you simply watch the students’ reactions; they might be there because of the teacher’s fame, or simply because it’s convenient—close by, right time, right location, etc. However, online courses inevitably compete with many other high-quality offerings. If one doesn’t take it seriously, it’s impossible to gain the trust of followers.
