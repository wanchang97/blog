# Foreword: An Unexpected Surprise
This winter, I spent two "chill" weeks in Serbia with my mom. From wandering around Belgrade to a spontaneous week of skiing in Kopaonik, this trip—originally spurred by visa issues—ended up being full of unexpected delights.
The cost of living here is incredibly low, and contrary to rumors of unrest, it’s filled with a kind of righteous sense of security unique to socialist countries.

# History and Reality: Walking the Tightrope Post-Conflict
The past century has been marked by endless wars, religious strife, and competing empires. You can see "Kosovo is Serbia" slogans as soon as you step off the plane, as if a single spark could reignite the conflict. Since the 2008 Kosovo independence war, the border remains a source of contention.

Some staggering data: the capital, Belgrade, has experienced 115 wars in its history and was razed to the ground 44 times—an occurrence unique in urban history worldwide. Walking through Belgrade's old town, you cannot ignore the black marks on building facades and the truncated branches. Not all of these tell stories of artillery fire. Some are indeed the charred scars left by the 1999 airstrikes, still standing in the city center like frozen pain. However, most are the result of a century of soot, exhaust, and oxidation over time. These varying shades of gray and black, clinging to exquisite Austro-Hungarian carvings, make the city look like a faded charcoal drawing—a sight quite different from the quaint, elegant European towns one is used to. I shudder to imagine what Ukraine, still in the midst of war, looks like.

But at the same time, because it sits at the crossroads of East and West, the city seamlessly integrates three major religions and tolerates differing political views. Modern Serbia is playing a delicate game between multiple global powers. While internal voices are diverse, maintaining strong ties with China and Russia while staying open to the EU and the US provides a strong engine for economic growth. Not to mention that Serbia has been visa-free for Chinese citizens with ordinary passports since 2017. They are actively seeking EU membership while maintaining friendliness with the US—for example, in late 2025, the Serbian parliament voted 130-40 to approve a proposal to build a Trump hotel on the site of the former Yugoslav Ministry of Defense building bombed by NATO. The project, backed personally by President Vučić, was declared an emergency project and stripped of its cultural heritage status, causing strong dissatisfaction and protests among locals. Whether this represents a lack of principles and a failure to protect history, or flexible pragmatic governance focused on economic development, opinions vary. It is true that museums and history preservation here lack the rigid conservation traditions seen in Western Europe, often requiring multiple protests to push the government toward restoration. But how can I criticize a nation that has been constantly ravaged by war and forced to relocate for not knowing how to "hold onto" its culture? Furthermore, many now argue that Europe is generally too rigid and slow to adapt, leading to sluggish growth.

Setting aside current affairs, I have compiled some practical travel tips for those planning a "spur-of-the-moment" trip. The [Chenhe's Blog - Complete Serbia Skiing Guide](https://chenhe.me/zh/posts/skiing-in-serbia/) was incredibly helpful during my planning. Inspired by that, I’d like to build on it with my own experiences from early 2026 to offer some updated insights for those planning a trip to Serbia.

# Basic Travel Guide to Serbia: Preparation

## 🛂 Entry and Visa
**Visa-free for Chinese passport holders.** The process is incredibly smooth. There is essentially no queue and no difficulty; your passport is stamped upon entry. Serbia is a non-Schengen country in the Balkans, so you do not need a Schengen visa if flying directly from China.

## 📶 Network and Data
### International Roaming (from Domestic Carriers) — Not Recommended
1. **Signal issues:** Roaming signals are unstable and often downgrade.
2. **The "Great Firewall" persists:** Domestic roaming cannot directly open Google Maps, ChatGPT, or Gemini, which is fatal for independent travel (map checking/real-time translation).
It is better to connect to airport Wi-Fi and purchase a local SIM card there.

### International eSIM Plan — Convenient and Flexible, Slightly More Expensive
Suitable for multi-country trips. Most Chinese phones have dual physical SIM slots and don't support eSIM, while American versions are often virtual-only. European phones usually have one physical and one virtual slot. Please check your phone type for eSIM compatibility.

### Purchasing a Local SIM or eSIM
* **Channels:** After arriving in Serbia, you can find counters for carriers like **Yettel** or **A1** in the baggage claim area. Prices are transparent, and they activate your SIM on-site using your passport.
* **Package Reference (February 2026):**
    * **Serbia Only:** For example, Yettel offers 100GB for 700 RSD for 15 days, or 50GB for 500 RSD for 7 days.

* **Calls:** Most packages are data-only. To contact hosts, download the carrier's app to top up for voice minutes or use WhatsApp via data.

## 🚌 Local Transportation
* **Airport Bus (A1 Shuttle):** Requires an additional fee (about 400 RSD/person) to reach the city center. The vehicles are older but fast.
* **Public Transit:** **Since January 1, 2025, public transport in Belgrade is completely free** (including for tourists). Buses are frequent; though crowded, they are very convenient.
* **Long-Distance Bus:** Google Maps often fails to show intercity transport, showing only driving or walking routes. Intercity buses are operated by dozens of private companies (e.g., Lasta, Jugoprevoz, Fudeks). These companies do not provide schedules in the formats Google requires.

    2. [Local bus site](https://www.polazak.rs/sr/), where you can book directly.     3. **Kopaonik Express:** Aside from regular buses, there are private shared transfers (e.g., Terra Travel or Gea Tours). They offer door-to-door service from your Belgrade accommodation. These don't show on map apps; you must book via their website, phone, or WhatsApp.
* **Taxis:** Use **Yandex Go** or **Pink Taxi**. Prices are transparent, avoiding tourist scams.

## 💰 Currency and Payment
The Serbian currency is the Dinar (**RSD**), with a relatively stable exchange rate against the Euro. Euros are also accepted, but cash is preferred, especially for B&Bs, so carry enough.
* **Strategy:** Bring **Euro cash** + **multi-currency credit card**.
* **Exchange:** There are automated exchange machines in the airport baggage claim with transparent rates.
* **Cash-only scenarios:** Airport buses, long-distance buses, taxis, some Booking.com rentals, ski gear rental, ski instructors, and small restaurants.
* **Budget reference:** 500 EUR in RSD cash is usually sufficient for two people for two weeks.

## 🧳 Packing: Onion-style dressing and German-style round-pin plugs
* **Climate:** Winters are mostly above 0°C, but the wind is fierce! At the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers, the cyclonic winds are wilder than at the beach.
* **Attire:** Recommend "onion-style" layering; bring everything from short sleeves to shell jackets.
* **For Skiers:** Recommend bringing professional hip and knee protectors. Avoid "Taobao turtle" pads as they shift easily and lack protection. Belgrade has a few Decathlons, but professional gear shops are scarce; rely on online shopping for quality gear.

# Belgrade Eats and Sightseeing: Markets, Ball Games, and the Yugoslav Drama Theatre
I'll skip the standard historical AI-generated info and focus on my personal favorites. We aimed for a local experience, staying in local apartments (not hotels/resorts), using public pools, visiting local bakeries, and farmers' markets. We walked or took the bus everywhere.

## 🏠 Accommodation
I booked two different apartments in Belgrade via Booking.com; they were excellent value. 

Belgrade is divided by the Sava River into the old city (East) and new city (West).

| Area | Characteristics | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Old Town (East)** | Crowded but lively, like Barcelona. Near sights, markets, shopping. | Those who love local bustle and walking. |
| **New City (West)** | Spacious, modern. Ušće Park, stadiums, new residential areas. | Those who like quiet, space, or business travel. |

#### Tašmajdan Pool
We visited this local pool. 600 RSD/person for 3 hours. It closes at noon and has morning/afternoon sessions. Fitness/Spa access is 2200 RSD/person.
Surprisingly, although the pool is Olympic-sized (50m), people don't swim the long length; everyone occupies a short lane and laps the 25m width.

## Culture and Performance
### JDP (Yugoslav Drama Theatre)
Check the schedule at [https://www.jdp.rs/](https://www.jdp.rs/). If online tickets are sold out, check the box office in person. The productions are avant-garde and immersive; even if you don't understand the Serbian, the physicality, music, and stagecraft are captivating.

### Museums
- The Museum of Contemporary Art (the jagged building in Ušće Park) is free on the first Wednesday of every month and on May 18th.
- The National Museum of Serbia is free every Sunday.

## Food and Specialties
#### Farmers' Markets and Bakeries
- **Kalenić Green Market:** Try local homemade **Kajmak** (milk cream) and **Ajvar** (pepper spread).
- **Zemun Farmers Market:** Great for fresh fish.
- **Pekara (Bakery):** Grab a sesame-covered Đevrek (bagel), very similar to Turkish Simit, paired with a small pink bottle of local yogurt. It’s the soul of a local breakfast.

#### Local Dining
- **Iva New Balkan Cuisine:** A Michelin-recommended spot offering creative twists on local Serbian meat/dairy dishes. Moderately priced; book at least a day in advance.
- **Langouste:** A Michelin-starred restaurant. Requires booking; no-shows incur a 3000 RSD penalty.

#### River Fish Restaurants in Zemun
- [**Šaran**](https://www.google.com/maps/place/%D0%A8%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD/@44.848099,20.4027304,2023m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x475a65ac3af50113:0x73a1d3a3253f267a!8m2!3d44.8480995!4d20.4130087!16s%2Fg%2F1tcwvhtc?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MDIwMy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D): A century-old restaurant. # Kopaonik Skiing: Beginner's Guide
My advice is tailored for beginners, as we spent our week mostly on the "magic carpet" conveyer belts. With an instructor, we managed two mountain runs and one night ski. Both my mom and I are cautious, and since it was our first time (Mom on skis, me on a snowboard), we learned a lot the hard way.

## Insurance
### Travel Insurance
Insurance needs vary. Since my mom was already in Germany before our trip, standard Schengen insurance wouldn't cover travel to Serbia. We found [Heymondo](https://heymondo.com/) which covers travelers who are "already abroad" (activates 72 hours after purchase). For long-term European residents, [check24](https://www.check24.de/reiseversicherung/?c9_dr=bd5efdb112e49406478f) is a good tool to compare.

### Ski Insurance
- **Ski Pass:** Usually includes basic insurance for the day. 
- **DSV (Deutsche Ski Verein):** Excellent for frequent skiers in Europe. It covers global ski travel risks, gear loss, and third-party liability.
- **Gear Rental:** Sometimes comes with optional insurance. If you have DSV, you don't need extra rental shop insurance.

## Accommodation & Gear
For beginners, stay near **Ski Center Kopaonik** to be close to the nursery slopes. 
- **InfoKop:** The go-to site for weather, lift status, ski pass prices, and last-minute accommodation. 
- **Mašinac Resort:** Located right next to the #22 green slope. A hostel-like spot that's often fully booked but is the cheapest for gear rental and instructors.
- **Brzeće Village:** Cheaper than the ski center, connected by a new gondola. Last gondola is at 16:00; miss it and you'll need the shuttle bus.

## Ski Pass and Instructors
- Buy passes on [InfoKop](https://www.infokop.net/) for a 5% discount.
- Night skiing is on slope #7—great conditions and atmosphere.
- Beginners should stick to the magic carpet area. Normal instructor rates are around 45 EUR/hour. Avoid "cheap" offers from uncertified instructors who might try to sell you someone else's ski pass; it's illegal and risky.

## Relaxation: Spa & Wellness
After the physical toll, treat yourself to the [Gorski Hotel](https://www.gorskihotel.com/files/wellness-and-spa-gorski.pdf?v1). Non-guests can pay 3900 RSD/person for a half-day pass. The B1 floor also hosts an Italian Fine Dining restaurant worth trying!
