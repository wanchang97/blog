I am an objective materialist. I believe in science, reason, and empiricism.

However, I am also cautious: blindly worshiping science is itself a form of "superstition"—and by "superstition," I mean accepting any doctrine in its entirety without question.

My attitude toward the ultimate laws of life is this: **I believe in the existence of a fundamental "Tao," but all human doctrines (such as science, theology, philosophy, psychology, and metaphysics) are merely models approximating it.** Some models are crude and cannot withstand scrutiny; others are precise and irrefutable. But in any discipline, researchers are driven by curiosity and imagination to build models they find credible.

To me, astrology is simply one such model.

---

### Part 1: Why do I "believe" in astrology?

The connection between star charts and people—between personality and fate—holds a mysterious attraction for me. I am often reminded of the "Twelve Beauties of Jinling" in *Dream of the Red Chamber*: **Each person's personality and destiny are, in fact, metaphorically embedded in their nature.**

Or consider the premise of the Pixar film *Soul*:
> Before coming to Earth, our souls spent time in various "seminar rooms." Selfishness, courage, peace... the amount of time spent in each forms the base color of our inherent personality. Later, we head to the "Hall of Everything" to find our "Spark"; that passion is the key to a successful incarnation.

![Soul - Finding your spark](/blog/images/astrology/soul2.png)

**To me, astrology is more like a "factory settings manual":**
It is not a final verdict on destiny, but a reference prototype for personality.

---

### Part 2: My curiosity and exploration

Like many others, I started learning about astrology when I was caught in emotional confusion. Questions like "Does he like me?" or "Are we a good match?" pushed me toward astrological interpretation.

I have watched almost every video by Master Tang Qiyang and learned that everyone has not only a Sun sign, but also Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto placements. **Each person possesses a unique star chart.**

> **[Model Definition]: Star Chart**
> 
> It is a snapshot of the positions of the stars at the exact moment of a person's birth.
> Because celestial bodies are constantly in motion, a difference of just a few minutes can lead to entirely different meanings.

This "one-of-a-kind" explanation resonates with my worldview. However, when I searched for professional literature (such as Google Scholar or astronomical research), I could not find arguments that convinced me.

Thus, I encountered the common contradiction faced by a rationalist exploring metaphysics: **the desire to explore tempered by the fear of becoming lost.**

---

### Part 3: My "irrational" investment

Professor Yang Zhao is a historian I admire and the pride of us Aries. I spent a significant amount of money to sign up for his new course, *A Reasonable Astrology Class*, this year.

This serves as my formal exploration into an "unlegitimized" field. It is interesting that I hesitate to spend money on things I firmly believe in, like AI or psychology, yet I invest in metaphysics. I often reflect: **Is this a form of escapism from the pressures of reality, studies, and work?**

But on an emotional level, I have a suspicion that cannot be proven: **Perhaps even in the uncertainty of an undefined world, one can have imaginative and rational discussions.**

Yet, studying astrology seems to lead to an inescapable dilemma:
1. Is this the most accurate reading of me?
2. How should I view this "verdict" of fate?

I still remember the shock of seeing Rebecca in *Imperfect Us* realize before her death that her birthday was not what she thought it was. The entire logic of the series was built on the two protagonists sharing the same birthday and personality, leading to their entangled lives. It was unexpected to find the foundation of this structure was fake—the two were not even the same sign; Rebecca didn't know her birth date, and her parents had just filled it in randomly.

Or, for example, as someone born on the cusp of Aries and Taurus, I had always thought of myself as a Taurus, only to suddenly discover I was actually an Aries. All the previous rationalizations suddenly felt ridiculous.

---

### Part 4: Why am I "afraid to go deeper"?

Although I believe astrology has some explanatory power, I always keep my distance:
* **If I see disaster (e.g., Saturn return):** I fear living in constant anxiety, unable to enjoy the present.
* **If I see good omens:** I fear setting my expectations too high, worrying that I will fail to live up to the luck promised.

I often contradictorily hope to meet a highly accurate master, but one who doesn't tell me *everything*. Because I still want to retain the joy and courage of exploration while in a state of unknowing. Even if my life were already coded, I still want to hold onto the illusion—the illusion that I arrived here through my own choices and agency.

I am also reminded of the setting in Isaac Asimov's *Foundation* series:
> To save the Galactic Empire and shorten the era of decline before a revival, the scientists preserving the essence of human civilization were placed on the First Foundation. They are responsible for preserving and advancing human culture and technology. However, one group of scientists—the Psychohistorians (psychologists who use mathematical statistics to predict the overall fate of humanity)—had to be hidden away on the Second Foundation. Because once the subjects know their fate, they would rebel or correct it, rendering the prediction useless.
 
What I crave, perhaps, is to maintain the distance between the First and Second Foundations: even if life is already encoded, I want to preserve the illusion, the illusion that **it was through my own choices and agency that I arrived at this point.**

---

### Part 5: If you know your fate, how should you live?

Although I am still far from understanding star charts well enough to build my own model, if I were truly gifted and saw through the mysteries of the universe, how could I maintain my vitality? I think of **Xiangling** from *Dream of the Red Chamber*. She was someone whose tragic fate was predicted early on. Her father, Zhen Shiyin, was lucky enough to meet the "Scabby-Headed Monk" and the "Limping Taoist" in both his dreams and reality—people who truly saw through the heavenly secrets. But what they told him were tragedies we would desperately avoid. His daughter was described as "having fate but no luck, a creature that would exhaust her parents." Later, she was kidnapped by human traffickers, and her parents suffered one misfortune after another. After passing through many hands, Yinglian finally met a reliable fiancé, only to be seized by the tyrant Xue Pan before the marriage, then left ignored and unloved.

![Xiangling learning poetry](/blog/images/astrology/xiangling.png)

Xiangling's fate was indeed as bumpy and rocky as the monk had described. But what touches me is the time she spent in the Prospect Garden with Daiyu and Baochai, forgetting to eat and sleep to learn poetry. Regardless of how her life or fate trampled on her, she always held onto her pursuit and persistence. No matter the status she held, after twists and turns, she arrived at the place that belonged to her; fate ultimately cherished her.

> **In those moments of obsession with writing poetry, she was no one's maid; she was simply someone who fully enjoyed life.**

Adversity and prosperity are general trends, difficult for human effort to stop; but our **stance** toward fate is always our own choice.

---

### Conclusion

For now, I maintain an "ambiguous" relationship with astrology: when I see favorable descriptions, I keep them as positive suggestions for action; when I see bad predictions, I remind myself that fortune and misfortune are intertwined, and challenges are not necessarily bad things.

I want to maintain an open mind to explore my personality traits, but I don't want to become a fortune-teller. I secretly look forward to predicting the future, but I believe even more: **Fate has no inherent good or evil; blessing and misfortune are both of our own making.**

What matters is to preserve that courage, just like Xiangling. In a fickle fate, look for the poetry that belongs to you.
