### Introduction: Signals from the Body

My allergies have returned. What is the cause? Is it really allergens and the weather? Or is it here to protect me from something, or to remind me of something?

### 1. Tension, Fantasy, and Oppression

Whenever I feel tense, I often fantasize about "how great it would be if I were incredibly rich," thinking it would stop me from putting so much pressure on myself. For example, buying a huge breakout stock or making some brilliant investment decision.

Sometimes I desperately want to make money, to have my own career, a stable income, and social recognition; at other times, I feel completely disillusioned. It takes so much courage and luck to find one's niche and stick to it bravely. If one can make money from just a little stock trading, then those who go all-in, those who have held positions for years, and those who are major shareholders in their own companies would be earning immeasurable wealth.

If the whole society were like this, with the gap between the rich and poor becoming increasingly polarized, would anyone still live under oppression? Wouldn't the oppressed feel disillusioned?

What is an unfree, oppressed life? It is sticking to a job you don't like, chasing what seems like a blank check of a promise, and exhausting your time and energy. Like a donkey walking in circles grinding grain, constantly chasing a carrot that seems just within reach but remains forever at a distance. Is that not my current state?

### 2. The Miracle Question: When Money Is No Longer an Issue

If money were truly no longer an issue for me (that is, the day I have a constant, self-sustaining source of income)...

Would I be happy sitting on a pile of money? Perhaps I would feel more confident due to a sense of accomplishment.

Would my food consumption change? I might stop buying discounted items and start pursuing higher standards. For my three daily meals, I would judge them only by their health, freshness, and the chef's dedication. Even if I felt a bit shy when visiting high-end restaurants, I would feel more at ease internally and wouldn't negate my own worth because of a waiter's attitude. Food would become more of a connection to the natural environment and a unique cultural experience, like the jujube paste yam cakes, sour bamboo shoot chicken skin soup, or bean skin buns from *Dream of the Red Chamber*.

How would my daily habits change? Overall, I would hope for more experiences. I might have familiar little communities in different regions, similar to 'digital nomad' living, but not aimless drifting. I would have a main thread to my life, but could often wander without a destination. I would have fresh experiences while maintaining a familiar rhythm, and flight booking decisions wouldn't be affected by peak or off-peak seasons. I could better satisfy the idealistic child inside me who wants to 'go at a moment's notice'—whenever there's an opportunity, I must act, no longer finding excuses to avoid living the life I want due to a lack of money or time.

How would I manage my time? Most likely, I would still want to exercise every morning—running, Yoga, swimming, or table tennis if possible. But I might spend a significant amount to hire trainers I trust for occasional guidance, because I prefer the rhythm of 'seeking periodic advice, but practicing on my own.' I might pick up brand new, less active hobbies, such as painting, photography, or music.

Would I still teach Yoga? I might hope to improve through teaching. But I would only start classes when the timing is right and the destiny aligns. I wouldn't want to be an instructor to make a living, nor would I want to aggressively promote the benefits of Yoga. I would outsource video production and organizational tasks to people I trust, focusing only on the parts I'm truly interested in.

Would I still pursue a PhD? Maybe. But not for the sake of graduation; I would study purely out of interest. I would study where I could truly learn something, rather than to find a job or obtain residency status. Moreover, the subjects wouldn't be limited to mature academic fields—botany, astrology, metaphysics, religion, anthropology, psychological mathematics, microbiology, etc., would all be on the table. I would hope to discuss things with people who are truly passionate about those fields, rather than those concerned with professional titles or salary.

What would my relationship with work be like? My work would serve me; it would be a source of value and satisfaction. I could leave at any time and express my true thoughts boldly. For repetitive, unnecessary tasks, I might hire people I trust to handle them. I might have a team I trust; I would know the general direction of our work, while they handle the specific details. I would only do what I am truly interested in, and even if those details still consumed my time and energy, it would be a process of artistic creation and spiritual practice.

How would my social circle change? I still hope for sincere communication, while hoping to get along with more diverse people. Although the social circles and living spheres of the wealthy can also be quite limited (e.g., only flying on their own private jets, never waiting at an airport), I feel that, as someone currently lacking internally, I cannot fully open up to friends. I crave self-growth more and have utilitarian, selfish time arrangements, fearing that if I miss a moment, it will affect my potential career, so I don't dare stop. Socializing is faked, and 'relaxation' is also faked. The first great luxury I could trade for after reaching income freedom is that I could truly be relaxed, not performatively relaxed. I might instead spend more time chatting with friends. Not to show off my life, but finally feeling that I have let myself off the hook and hoping my friends can do the same.

What kind of books would I read? Or would I stop reading once I'm rich and just fly to the author for a chat? Just kidding. This probably wouldn't change much, as even with money, I can't meet Cao Xueqin or Márquez. So I would still read silently, read with excitement, read in secret, reading every book I'm interested in, and I could close any book I don't want to read at any time. However, I probably wouldn't 'check in' on WeChat Reading to earn free points anymore. After all, if I'm that rich, I'd just buy the annual pass.

### 3. Limitations and Epiphanies

What limitations would I still face? Even with money, I still can't buy sincerity and love; they still require courage and honesty to protect. My health would likely improve due to a prosperous life and mental relaxation, but I still couldn't escape the unpredictability of life. My time would still pass incessantly. I am aging, becoming slower, constantly saying goodbye to those around me, and there are still many regrets and things left undone.

So, to me, money means security and freedom. What I want more is perhaps not money itself, but the inner spaciousness and composure it brings; it is my self-confidence and certainty, the freedom to design and arrange my own life without needing to explain to anyone, without guilt, and without looking over my shoulder, afraid to be different.

For me, money can largely be traded for time and freedom. However, I do not wish to exhaust my time and surrender my freedom in the process of chasing that money. In other words, if I am internally certain, no longer anxious about not working hard enough, and maintain the right to choose how I spend my time and freedom, have I not already been able to live, or enjoy, the life of a rich person? Or even if I achieve my financial goals and become a 'rich person,' what kind of rich person would I be? A rich person enslaved by money, becoming more fearful the wealthier they get, or a 'wealthy loafer' like Jia Baoyu?

Would I even remember what kind of life I originally wanted by then? If, when I finally have money, my body and energy have faded, what experience can money provide me?

### 4. The Ultimate Decision: Life Quality and 'Die with Zero'

Therefore, a new type of financial philosophy, 'Die with Zero,' is very attractive to me—spending all your money before you die, so that every penny fulfills its intended purpose, whether through donation or consumption.

$$ \text{Life Value} = \sum (\text{Pure experiences and spiritual energy acquired within limited time}) $$

Truly, the only scarce resources are time and energy. For everyone, they are finite; they can only diminish, and they cannot be reversed. It is just that time cannot be exchanged or circulated; otherwise, time would probably be the hardest currency. All other resources can be acquired if one invests more effort and time.

Therefore, it is truly a pity to lose one's time for the sake of money (an ingenious rule of capitalism that makes social resources run more efficiently, but also creates great unfairness).

The choices we make, at every moment, should always be: Is this something I am willing to trade my life for? Does this investment of time and energy enhance the quality and energy of my life?

If the answer is yes, then that is the most rational decision for you.
